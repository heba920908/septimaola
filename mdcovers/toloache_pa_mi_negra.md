## Performance Notes
- **Style:** Fast ska upstroke pattern with characteristic off-beat emphasis on beats 2 and 4; the horn section carries the melodic weight while the rhythm section drives an insistent two-beat ska pulse
- **Bass lines:** The bass locks tightly with the drums on a root-fifth ska pattern throughout. For each chord, play root on beat 1 and fifth on the "and" of 2: B→F# over the B bar, D#→A# over D#m, C#→G# over C#m, F#→C# over F#m. The walking descent B–D#m–C#m creates a chromatic drop from D# to C# that gives the groove its forward momentum — nail the voice-leading between those two chords to make the pocket feel effortless
- **Special arrangements:** The intro features the horn riff doubled by guitar before the full band enters. The "ska..." exclamation in the bridge signals the band to briefly accent the off-beats even harder (classic Panteon Rococó style)
- **Instrumental breaks:** The tag ("aha aha aha aha") is usually played as a two-bar break with the bass holding on B–D#m, giving space for the crowd call-and-response before the final chorus repetition
- **Key changes:** None — the four-chord loop B–D#m–C#m–F#m remains constant from intro to outro. The F#m (minor v) is a borrowed chord from B minor, giving the progression a slightly tense, unresolved quality that suits the "love potion" desperation theme
- **Dynamics:** Keep the intro tight and punchy; let the energy build verse by verse; the chorus should burst with full horn accents and maximum ska upstroke intensity
